# Research data sources

Checked October 2026. Prices, limits and features change often: confirm on the provider's own page before quoting a cost, and say "as of `<month year>`".

## Free sources

| Source | What it gives | Limits | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Search Console | Real queries, pages, clicks, impressions and positions for your own site, up to 16 months | Your site only; some queries anonymised; no competitor data | Verified property; user signs in or exports |
| Bing Webmaster Tools | Your site's Bing queries; a keyword research tool; inbound links | Bing data only, smaller samples | Verified site (can import from Search Console) |
| Google Trends | Relative interest over time and by region; compares phrasings; rising topics | Index from 0 to 100, not search volumes | Nothing |
| Google Keyword Planner | Keyword ideas and volume estimates | Needs a Google Ads account; users without active ad spend commonly report seeing broad ranges rather than exact figures (not documented as policy) | Google Ads account (no need to run ads) |
| Manual SERP review | Page types, intent, features, who ranks | Personalised by location and history; time-consuming | Private browser window, location set |
| People Also Ask, autocomplete, related searches | The questions and phrasings people use | Changes as you click; no volumes | Nothing |
| Site search logs and enquiry emails | What visitors could not find; customer language | Needs permission; remove personal data | Analytics or inbox access, with approval |

Advantages: first-party or Google-sourced; nothing about your plans goes to a third party; no cost. Disadvantages: no competitor keyword lists or backlink indexes; slower; volume figures are coarse.

## Paid sources

| Type | Examples | What it adds | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All-in-one SEO suites | Ahrefs, Semrush, similar tools (SE Ranking, Moz, Similarweb and others) | Competitor keyword lists, ranking estimates, keyword difficulty, backlink indexes, site audits, rank tracking | Figures are modelled from panels and crawls, not Google data; different tools disagree; annual plans and auto-renewal; seat and project limits |
| Pay-per-use APIs | Keyword and SERP data providers | Bulk data at per-request prices; scriptable | Credits drain fast in loops; still estimates; API keys to protect |
| Backlink specialists | Link index tools | Deeper link histories | Overlaps with suites |

Costs as of October 2026, from third-party reports (confirm on vendor pages): Ahrefs Lite around US$129 per month billed monthly (a cheaper Starter tier also exists); Semrush Pro around US$139.95 per month billed monthly; annual billing is cheaper per month. Free tiers and trials exist but are limited and may require card details: the user must start them, not the agent.

## Choice prompt

Ask first:

1. Is there any budget for tools, and is it one-off or monthly?
2. Do you already pay for an SEO tool, or have Search Console and Google Ads accounts?
3. How competitive is the market: are the top results big national brands or local specialists?
4. Is it acceptable for a third-party tool to hold your domain, competitor list and project data?

Then offer, for example:

> **How should we gather the data?**
> 1. **Free only** (Search Console, Trends, Keyword Planner, manual SERP review). Accurate for your own site, no cost, slower, no competitor keyword lists. Best if budget is tight or the market is local.
> 2. **One month of a paid suite** (about US$130–140). Fast competitor and backlink view; numbers are estimates; remember to cancel. Best for a one-off strategy in a crowded market.
> 3. **Keep the paid suite you already have.** No new cost; use it for competitor context, Search Console for your own figures.
>
> I recommend option `<n>` because `<reason from their answers>`.

Wait for the answer before doing anything that needs an account or payment.

## Recording evidence

Every number in the strategy carries: source, date collected, location and device (for SERP observations), and type:

- **Observed**: you saw it (a SERP feature, a competitor page type).
- **First-party**: from the organisation's own Search Console or analytics.
- **Estimate**: from a tool's model (search volume, difficulty, competitor traffic).
- **Assumption**: reasoning without data; say what would confirm it.
