# SEO research and strategy

Use when the request concerns opportunity discovery or strategic prioritisation rather than implementation of one page.

## Scope the evidence

Confirm business model, audience, geography, offerings, conversion goals, current site, known competitors, available analytics/search data, budget, and decision horizon. Separate public observations, first-party measurements, paid-provider metrics, estimates, and assumptions.

Offer data-source choices before using a paid API, consuming credits, authenticating an account, or accessing private analytics. State expected value, cost exposure, requested permissions, stored outputs, and a free/manual fallback.

## Select only relevant modes

- Search opportunity and competitors: demand, intent, SERP/page-type patterns, content gaps, and differentiation.
- Backlinks: referring-domain quality, topical relevance, anchors, lost/gained links, risk signals, and realistic acquisition opportunities.
- Local/maps: business type, service area, public identity consistency, profile/category fit, reviews, citations, and geographic competitors.
- Ecommerce: product/category architecture, merchant visibility, product data, marketplace competition, and truthful product schema needs.
- International: language/region demand and market-specific content requirements.

## Output

Produce a prioritised strategy with evidence source/date, confidence, expected user/business value, prerequisites, owner, effort, risk, and success measure. Do not use a single score as a substitute for findings. Do not recommend mass page generation, link manipulation, invented reviews, or unverifiable claims.
