# SEO content system

Use when work concerns content quality, page intent, briefs, editorial architecture, comparison pages, topic clusters, scalable templates, or search/AI answer usefulness.

## Start from the visitor decision

Determine the question, task, expected page type, audience, evidence needs, unique contribution, conversion path, and likely alternatives. Review actual search-result patterns when current search evidence is necessary.

## Choose one content mode

- Existing-page assessment: usefulness, accuracy, authorship, evidence, freshness, readability, structure, and next action.
- Content brief: purpose, audience, questions, evidence, outline, internal links, media, metadata direction, reviewer, and acceptance criteria.
- Topic system: group subjects by shared intent and observed search overlap; define hub/support relationships and avoid duplicate pages.
- Comparison/alternatives: use fair criteria, verifiable claims, dated evidence, disclosed limitations, and a useful decision path.
- Scaled pages: require a trustworthy data source, meaningful per-page uniqueness, index-control plan, template QA, and a stop condition for thin output.
- Search experience: compare the proposed page type and journey with what the query appears to reward; correct intent mismatch before polishing keywords.
- AI-search readiness: make important claims attributable, specific, structured, current, and easy to verify without weakening ordinary search or accessibility.

## Guardrails

Do not invent expertise, citations, statistics, customer outcomes, locations, comparisons, or product facts. Do not treat keyword-density targets, AI-detector scores, or generated prose as quality evidence. High-risk charity, health, legal, financial, and safeguarding content requires an identified reviewer and authoritative sources.

Return a content plan or assessment with owner, evidence, freshness trigger, internal links, next action, and observable acceptance test.
